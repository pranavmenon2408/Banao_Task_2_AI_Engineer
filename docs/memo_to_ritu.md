# Memo: warranty fraud — what to do next week

**To:** Ritu Deshpande, Head of D2C Operations  **Cc:** Farhan Sheikh, Meenal Joshi, Tanmay Kulkarni
**Re:** Fraud flag on warranty claims

## The decision

Put the fraud score live **as a ranked review list for the investigation desk**. In the same week, **take seven outlets off auto-approval**.

Please don't report "accuracy above 97%" to the board. A system that flags nothing already scores 98.7%, because only about 1 claim in 80 is fraud. That number would look good while stopping nothing.
Report instead **rupees of fraud stopped per claim checked** (the measure Farhan asked for) and **the share of fraud rupees caught**.

## The numbers

- **Fraud is about 2% of warranty payouts:** roughly ₹44,000 a month in a normal month, and ₹6.4 lakh over the 15 months we have.
- **In normal months the score works well.** We tested it on 9 past months, each scored blind. The desk would have needed only about **7 reviews a month (of its 40)**, and would have caught **76% of fraud rupees**: about **₹33,000 a month saved** after goodwill and contact costs.
- **Since 1 May the fraud has changed shape.** Claims under ₹2,000 are now auto-approved without inspection, and the fraud moved there. Each fraud is small, so a desk review barely pays for itself.
- **Most of it comes from seven outlets:** SP3286 (Mysuru), SP3118 and SP3160 (Pune), SP3129 (Chennai), SP3232 (Jaipur), SP3319 (Warangal) and SP3318 (Hubballi).
  - Since May, **33 of their 58 claims were confirmed fraud**.
  - For Jul–Sep they file about 4% of claims but carry **over half of the expected fraud**.

## On "the newer partners"

The data partly supports you, and Meenal is also right:
- Since May, the fraud sits with partners onboarded **3 to 8 months** earlier and using auto-approval.
- It is **10 of the roughly 60 new partners, not the group**. A "newest partners first" rule would have done worse than picking claims at random.
- Act on named outlets, not on a whole category of partner.

## What it's worth (Jul–Sep 2026, model estimates)

| Action | Per month |
|---|---|
| Expected fraud across all claims | ~₹27,000 |
| Desk works the ranked list (about 10 claims) | ~₹6,000 saved |
| Kestrel checks every claim from the seven outlets (about 32 claims at ₹260) | ~₹6,000 saved net |
| **Together** | **~₹12,000 a month, about ₹1.5 lakh a year**, before counting the fraud these outlets stop attempting |

If fraud returns to its pre-May pattern of fewer, larger claims, the desk list alone is worth about ₹33,000 a month.

## What the score can't do

About 4 in 10 frauds come from outlets with no fraud on record. On the information in a claim today, those look identical to genuine claims. **No model will catch them without new evidence**, such as checking the purchase invoice or matching serials against our sales records.

## Next week

1. **Monday:** suspend auto-approval for the seven outlets. Their claims get a Kestrel-side check (not the partner's own sign-off). Use about 7 spare desk slots to audit those outlets directly.
2. **From Monday:** the desk works the ranked list in the new review screen. Each flag comes with plain-English reasons.
3. **Board pack:** replace "accuracy" with "₹ fraud stopped per claim checked" and "% of fraud rupees caught".
4. **Tanmay:** record *when* each investigation closes, and stop any system saving "undecided" as "not fraud". Both limit how well we can measure.
5. **Monthly:** retrain the score on the latest outcomes. A new rule or a new outlet going bad takes about a month to show up.
